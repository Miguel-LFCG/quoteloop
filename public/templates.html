Before you follow up

Use the channel the customer already prefers. Refer to the job and the quote date, offer help with questions, and avoid invented deadlines, scarcity or pressure. If the customer says no or asks not to be contacted, stop.

1–2 days after sending

Use this when you want to check that the quote arrived and give the customer an easy way to ask questions.

Hi [name] — just checking you received the quote for [job]. Happy to answer any questions or clarify the options. Thanks, [your name]

5–7 days after sending

Use this when there has been no reply and you are planning upcoming work.

Hi [name], I’m planning the next few weeks and wanted to check whether you’d like me to hold a slot for [job]. No pressure if plans have changed — just let me know either way.

After a change or clarification

Use this when you have answered a question, changed the scope or sent an updated quote.

Hi [name] — I’ve attached the updated quote for [job] with [brief change]. Have a look when convenient and let me know if anything needs explaining.

After 14 days with no answer

A clear close-out message prevents an endless chase and leaves the door open.

Hi [name], I’ll close this quote for now so I don’t keep bothering you. If you’d like to revisit [job] later, just reply and I’ll pick it back up. All the best.

Keep the routine simple

  • Choose the next follow-up date before moving on to the next job.
  • Keep one short list of open quotes rather than relying on memory or scattered messages.
  • Record the outcome: won, lost, paused or follow up later.
  • Only follow up work you can genuinely deliver.

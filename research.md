# QuoteLoop founder research

_Date: 31 August 2026 · scope: UK independent trades, pilot only · outreach: none_

## Executive decision

Build **QuoteLoop**: a narrow quote-follow-up board plus a founder-led setup service for solo and small UK trades businesses. The product is not a full CRM and does not claim to increase revenue automatically. The initial job is smaller and testable: make the next human follow-up visible and easy.

Why this won: it sits on a painful, revenue-adjacent workflow; existing paid products prove willingness to pay; a useful local-first MVP can be built without paid APIs; and a £49 setup offer can reach cash faster than waiting for a polished multi-tenant SaaS. The main risk is not technical feasibility but crowded competition and whether tradespeople will change their current habit.

## Demand evidence

1. The Office for National Statistics reported 370,770 VAT/PAYE-registered construction firms operating across Great Britain in Q3 2024.[10] This is a large, fragmented pool; it is a market-size signal, not a claim that every firm is a prospect.
2. Powered Now's 2026 trades workforce report frames the admin burden directly: its page says 93% of tradespeople report stress or anxiety and shows 277 hours lost to admin each year.[1] This is commissioned industry research, so it is useful directional evidence rather than neutral proof of product demand.
3. Electrical Times' report of the same research says business admin — including quoting, invoicing and chasing payments — takes an average of five hours and 20 minutes per week.[2] That specifically includes the workflow QuoteLoop targets.
4. The pain is adjacent to money: the same report says tradespeople are owed an average of £5,901 in unpaid invoices.[2] QuoteLoop does not solve collections, but the evidence supports choosing revenue/admin workflows over a novelty product.
5. Existing paid products validate buying behaviour. Tradify lists £34 per user/month for Lite and a £10/month Instant Website add-on.[3] It includes quoting, invoicing, email tracking and other job-management features. This proves some trades businesses pay for admin software, while also showing that a broad suite is a strong incumbent.
6. Quote-Chaser is a direct competitor positioned around automatic quotation reminders and lists Pro at £9.99/month.[4] This is both validation and a warning: a generic "quote follow-up app" is already occupied. QuoteLoop therefore launches as a smaller, privacy-first, no-account board plus implementation help, not as a claim of category novelty.

The first row wins on speed-to-test and the ability to sell a service before building authentication, telephony or payment integrations. It loses on long-term defensibility; that is acceptable for a validation-first pilot.

## Exact customer

A UK sole trader or 2–5 person trade business (plumbing, electrical, heating, painting/decorating, carpentry, landscaping, roofing or property maintenance) that sends roughly 5–30 quotes a month and currently tracks them in WhatsApp, email, a notebook or a spreadsheet.

## Expensive problem

Warm quotes are sent, then buried under site work, calls and admin. The business spends time generating demand but does not have a reliable next-action habit. The cost is lost quoted work, avoidable admin stress and an inconsistent customer experience.

## One-sentence value proposition

**QuoteLoop gives busy UK tradespeople a two-minute daily list of the quotes that need a human follow-up, without making them adopt a bloated CRM.**

## Competitor wedge

- **Versus Tradify / Jobber-style suites:** narrower, no migration, no account, no per-user software bill for the free tool.
- **Versus Quote-Chaser and similar follow-up SaaS:** local-first privacy, manual human-in-the-loop messaging, a £49 setup service, and a workflow that can be tried immediately without a card.
- **Versus spreadsheets/notebooks:** due-today and overdue views, ready-to-edit scripts, exports and a mobile-friendly workflow.

The wedge is not feature superiority. It is lower friction and a paid implementation shortcut for a narrow persona. If customers ask for cloud sync, teams, automatic reminders or telephony, that is evidence for the next product version—not a reason to build them now.

## Falsification tests

- If 20 relevant conversations produce fewer than 5 people willing to try the free board, the workflow may not be painful enough.
- If 10 qualified pilot applications produce 0 paid £49 setups after fit review, kill or reposition the offer; do not add features first.
- If users open the app but do not add at least 5 quotes or return within 7 days, onboarding/habit is failing.
- If a pilot user reports no time saved, no better visibility or no useful customer replies after 14 days, stop charging and interview them.
- If competitors make the same wedge indistinguishable at a lower price, reposition around implementation or move to another admin leak.

## Evidence limitations

No cold outreach, paid ads, customer interviews or payment test was performed in this build window. Search engines were used only for ordinary public research; social pages were sometimes bot-blocked. Competitor positioning and commissioned reports are market signals, not proof that QuoteLoop will convert. The next evidence must come from real conversations and paid pilot decisions.
